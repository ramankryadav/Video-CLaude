# The POV money-story formula (POV Secret / Counter Finance)

This formula is proven on the user's scripts:
- Whitlock "Your Life at Every Generation of an Old Money Family" (118 boxes, about 31 min)
- "Your Landlord Was Old Money — 6 Rules" (123 boxes)
- "Grandpa Left Your Brother the House — 6 Old Money Rules" (102 boxes)
- "Your Dad Left at 9. He Came Back at $8 Million." (105 boxes)
- "Retired With $200K → $2 Million" (89 boxes, about 30 min)

The niche research behind it lives in the `pov-wealth-niche` memory.

## Who it's for
Older US viewers (40–70, retirees, tradespeople). They want relatable middle-class money (car payments, mortgages, retiring, family money fights), a person in conflict, and **real, honest math**. They fact-check in the comments.

## Structure (30 min ≈ 4,500 words ≈ 100 boxes; 20 min ≈ 3,000)

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Cold open (8–12 boxes)** | A **time-stamped scene**: "It's 9:40 on a Saturday morning…", "It's 3:30 on a Friday afternoon in October…". You're there, with **exact sensory detail** (a sheet cake with your name spelled wrong, a U-Haul double-parked, rain on an auction tent). The **doubter** speaks a quotable jab ("Two hundred grand, that's six years, buddy, maybe seven if you eat a lot of beans"). A loved one's small gesture ("she squeezes your hand under the table, twice"). Then the **thesis turn**: "Here's the thing about [doubter]'s math. He's right, if… But…" Close with **the promise** ("that one decision is going to turn… into…") and a **real statistic** that makes the viewer feel seen. |
| **Chapters by time or level (5–8)** | Headings like `## Part two · Henley's Hardware (2027)`, `## Generation three · The name (1975)` or `## Level 2 · Rule 2`. Each chapter moves the clock forward and shows the money in concrete numbers. |
| **The mentor** | An older, quietly rich character with a vivid look (green canvas apron, 2004 Ford Ranger, cardigan with elbow patches), a hidden fortune revealed slowly ("he says, very quietly…"), and **3–6 rules**, each a short quotable line ("The pile never pays a bill. Never sell in a storm. Never tell anyone the number."). |
| **The recurring object** | One physical motif that runs through the whole video and pays off at the end: the lunch pail with "H", rent receipts, the green recipe tin, a $20 bill, the yellow legal pad, the green apron. |
| **The doubter's parallel path** | The doubter makes the opposite choices and pays for them, shown with **real math** (Gary's withdrawals shrink his million). Not cartoonish: "nobody goes hungry in this story". |
| **Temptation and storm beats** | At least one moment where the hero almost breaks a rule (a roof bill, a crash, a family request), and the rule holds. |
| **Loss beat** | A gentle emotional low (a spouse dies, a mentor dies), with the object handed on. |
| **The reveal** | The number on a statement or document, a reaction ("asks to see it twice"), and then the **honest part** ("in 2026 money, this is worth roughly…"). |
| **Twist and callback** | A late reveal that reframes the start (Dana Brandt is the driver's great-granddaughter; the 27 sealed birthday cards; "rule three has an exception"). |
| **Closing reflection** | 2–3 boxes. A quotable lesson ("almost everyone eats their seed"), then a quiet last image ("Tomorrow is Tuesday, which means fishing"). |
| **Outro (2 boxes)** | "If this story changed how you think about…, hit like and subscribe… Tell me in the comments… [a specific question]. And this week, [one tiny actionable step]." Add "this is a story, not financial advice" when the video gives money mechanics. |

## Money and legal mechanics (pick 1–3 per video and explain them simply)
Paid-off house; Social Security timing (claiming at 62 cuts the check about 30% for life); bridge part-time work; Roth conversions (no required minimum distributions for the original owner; RMDs start at 75 for people born 1960 or later); the rule of 72; index funds; step-up basis; buy-borrow-die; an LLC per building; revocable, dynasty or incentive trusts; spendthrift clauses; seller financing; avalanche debt payoff; inherited Roth (10-year rule). **Verify every number with web search and compute it in Python before writing.**

## Writing rules
- **Second person, present tense** for "you". Name the other characters; "you" may be named in multi-generation stories (Henry, Walter…).
- **Numbers spoken in full:** "nine hundred and seventy-five dollars", "two point six million", "fifty-six hundred".
- **Concrete over abstract:** brand-free specifics ("a 2015 Camry with a hundred and forty thousand miles").
- **Titles** (2026 learning): short, person-in-conflict titles beat "POV:" prefixes. Families that win: a family snub then "worth $X", middle-class quiet wealth, retiring early, the mentor "Was Old Money — 6 Rules" (now flooded). Screen the title for similarity before writing.
- **Vids refusals:** Vids refused one box combining "$240K" and "worst night of your life". Keep a plain fallback for dark or money-heavy lines.
